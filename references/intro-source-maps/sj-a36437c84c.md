## Observed arrangements versus inferred purposes
Observed: two opening paragraphs precede three explicitly named challenges; method covers these in order but adds FirmSec preparation. Prior work is embedded in the relevant challenge, not forced into a separate survey paragraph. Two long contributions include the first detailed quantitative evaluation and impact counts. Before the list, P8.S8 previews only qualitative precision/recall; it does not supply the later benchmark scale or vulnerability counts.

Inferred purpose: the writer makes the whole translation chain the research object, so readers can judge why recovering specifications alone would not solve the deployed problem. The source-level baseline receives its enabling condition before its boundary, making the binary system's research value legible. Representational prerequisites are ordered before deployment checking; this reduces unexplained transitions while preserving three materially different content choices. These are editorial interpretations of observable sequencing, not claims about individual-author intentions.

## Transferable composing decisions
1. When a task contains dependent representations, state what one stage produces and why the next cannot consume it directly; then name the required intermediate
2. Place a plausible alternative beside the precise obstacle it could solve, acknowledge its real capability, and identify its input/coverage condition
3. Let artifact and measurement be separate contribution outcomes when the system also establishes prevalence and real-world consequence
4. A contribution can legitimately be long when it introduces evaluation detail not previewed earlier; do not copy its length into introductions that already contain an evaluation paragraph
