## Strong source claims versus scientific qualifications

The source strongly claims systemic and multidimensional vulnerabilities, effective ACI-Sentinel improvements, and interface standardization that avoids poor generalization from simplified/inconsistent environments. Current source-observation fields preserve those words and relations. Qualification is separate:

- The evaluated research objects are six integrated MAS implementations and three LLM configurations, with supported task domains; these are not direct security audits of the commercial products named in the opening and not a full six-by-four-by-three factorial guarantee
- The 1,356 cases pair benign tasks and attacks and are organized along three surfaces and objectives. This does not alone establish balanced or exhaustive coverage of all conceivable threat combinations
- Common interfaces and a consistent executor address a specific source of incomparable results; they cannot by themselves prove generalization to every deployment, model version or future task distribution
- BU, UA and ASR answer different questions. Low ASR without task utility does not establish useful robustness; no new global ranking is inferred from a flattened table
- ACI-Sentinel is an evaluated auxiliary defense/direction. §5.4 retains utility tradeoffs and failures; B.1 shows residual adaptive effectiveness; Limitations leaves robustness-oriented whole-system design for future work. These do not erase the source’s positive empirical claims
- The Introduction promises future public code and data, while p.1’s footnote says code is available. Both source signals are retained. This pass did not open the repository, verify code/data availability, licensing or reproducibility
- B.3 evaluates binary Disruption attack-success judgments on final responses. In the full suite, 585 tasks use the LLM judge and 771 use string matching. Its reliability check randomly samples 50 previously filtered-out benign tasks and subsequently combines them with attacks to include successes and failures; three humans annotate each case. The reported 98% is GPT-4o agreement with the majority human vote on that check. It is not a representative audit of the 585 judge-scored cases or all 1,356 cases, a validation of benign-task-selection scoring, or a MAS security rate. It offers narrowly scoped evidence about automatic Disruption judgment, not a replacement for substantive robustness findings
